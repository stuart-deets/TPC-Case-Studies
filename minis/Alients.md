# Background

In 2029, humanity detected and successfully established communication with an extraterrestrial intelligence through radio signals. The entity—temporarily designated "Partner"—appears to be carbon-based, lives on a distant planet, and has developed advanced spaceflight and scientific capabilities. Over the past six months, scientists have established a basic mathematical and scientific vocabulary with Partner using prime numbers, chemical formulas, and physical constants.

However, Partner perceives the universe fundamentally differently than humans. Partner has no visual system as we understand it—they navigate using what appears to be a combination of echolocation, magnetic field sensing, and chemical detection. Partner's body structure remains unknown, but communication suggests they may not have bilateral symmetry. Most critically, Partner has no concept of "left" and "right" as humans understand them.

## The Situation

Saint City Research Partners has been contracted by the International Space Communication Consortium (ISCC) to solve a critical problem. Scientists need to explain to Partner how to modify their spacecraft's trajectory for a planned rendezvous mission near Jupiter in 2031. The adjustment requires Partner to execute a "left turn" relative to their current heading.

Initial attempts to explain this have failed:

- "Rotate counterclockwise" assumes Partner shares our visual perspective and convention
- "Turn toward the lesser magnetic pole" fails because Partner's magnetic field orientation is reversed from Earth's
- "Orient yourself 90 degrees perpendicular to your current heading" doesn't specify which perpendicular direction
- Diagrams sent as image data were met with confusion—Partner appears unable to process or interpret visual representations the way we do

## Your Challenge

Develop a protocol to explain "left" to Partner using only:

1. Mathematical concepts (Partner understands numbers, geometry, algebra)
2. Physical phenomena (Partner understands gravity, magnetism, rotation, momentum, light wavelength)
3. Chemical markers (Partner can detect and differentiate chemical elements)
4. Astronomical references (Partner has provided star charts showing their location and can identify celestial objects)

Your protocol should:

- **Be unambiguous**: Cannot be misinterpreted as the opposite direction
- **Avoid anthropocentric assumptions**: Don't assume bilateral symmetry, visual perception, or cultural conventions
- **Be verifiable**: Include a way for Partner to confirm they understood correctly before the actual maneuver
- **Use shared knowledge**: Build only on concepts both species demonstrably understand

## Deliverable

Write a 200-300 word message to Partner that explains how to determine which direction is "left" relative to their current heading. Then, in a separate 100-150 word reflection, explain:

- What assumptions did you have to identify and avoid?
- What rhetorical choices did you make and why?
- How would you verify Partner understood correctly?
