# What Makes Technical Communication "Technical"?

Read the following two perspectives on what makes technical communication distinctive, based on this week's readings:

**Perspective 1 - Lippincott: "We Explain Things"**

Technical communicators fundamentally exist because "We explain things, and it is always the new that requires the greatest explanation." Our role centers on making complex, unfamiliar concepts accessible to audiences who need to understand and use them. A communicator, therefore, is responding to the needs and desires of others. The world is always already available to us, by the means of reason and science--all we must do (noting that it is quite the task) is to understand the world, and then transmit this information to others.

**Perspective 2 - Dobrin: "What's Technical About Technical Writing"**

Technical communication is "technical" because it deals with technologynot just tools or procedures, but "a way that people, machines, concepts and relationships are organized." We communicate about organized systems and structures. A communicator, therefore, not only responds to the world, but also actively in engaged in a fundamentally creative process of organizing the world (and being organized by the world, too, themselves).

Both of these ideas about technical writing focus on *technology* but have different relationships with it. Each of them are quite useful in our thinking about technical communication and technical writing. 

**Your Response:**

1. How do these two definitions complement each other? Where do they overlap? How are they different?
2. Using your field of study as an example, identify something "technical" you might need to communicate about. Does it fit better with Lippincott's "explaining the new" or Dobrin's "organized systems"or both?
3. Which perspective better captures what you think technical communication should prioritize: accessibility (explaining) or systematicity (organizing)? Why?
