One of the key tensions in Plain Language is between "simplifying" what is complex language, and maintaining the original meaning. While we discussed in class what I take to be the goal of the Plain Language movement--making communications that work for all audiences, but thinking especially of non-expert users and citizens--traditional definitions and understandings might take a different tack.

> [!Scenario]
> A defibrillator manufacturer must create warning labels for their new AED (Automated External Defibrillator). The original FDA-approved text reads: "Contraindicated for patients presenting with ventricular fibrillation refractory to pharmacological intervention when concurrent hypothermic conditions exist below 32°C core temperature." The safety team wants: "Don't use if patient is very cold and heart rhythm problems aren't responding to medicine." The legal team argues the simplified version could cause dangerous misinterpretation in emergency situations, while the safety team argues complex language could delay life-saving treatment when seconds matter.

For this activity, choose an abstract position of either or below:

1. It's better to simplify language and ideas so they are more accessible for more people.
2. It's better to keep complex ideas and language, because simplifying means that critical nuance is always lost, and leads to further confusion down the road.

Offer a justification for your side, in this case.
